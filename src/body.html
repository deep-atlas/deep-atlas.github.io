what changes
Sunlight and moonlight at the surface, and the light that reaches each depth. At dusk the lanternfish and their kin rise from the twilight zone to feed near the surface, and sink again before dawn: the largest migration on Earth, every night.

Depths and sizes are real; the horizontal layout is compressed so that every zone sits within a swim of the next. Fast motions are slowed or sped up to be seen, and the look of each creature is an artist's model. Below about 150 m the view is lit as a submersible's lamps would light it. Nautile is real; its route here is invented.

what's new

0.12ghosts, phantoms and spinners
  • A giant Pacific octopus, the largest octopus, crawls through the kelp forest.
  • A feather star swims over the reef on its beating arms. A giant frogfish twitches its lure. Yeti crabs at the vents now wave their arms, and cleaner shrimp work over the moray's open mouth.
  • Turtle hatchlings swim out to sea in their first frenzy, and a sea toad holds its breath on the deep floor.
  • A walrus swims under the Arctic ice with the polar bear and belugas. At night the parrotfish sleeps in a cocoon of mucus.
  • Spinner dolphins leap out of the waves and spin in the air: watch from above the surface.
  • A giant phantom jelly trails its 10 m curtain arms through the midnight zone.
  • On the abyssal plain: “Casper”, a ghostly white octopus first seen in 2016. In the trench, a supergiant amphipod, 34 cm long. Settings has a full screen button.
  • A mimic octopus on the sand: disturb it and it impersonates a poisonous flatfish, a lionfish, then a sea snake.
  • A family of sperm whales asleep, hanging upright just under the surface.
  • A pack of Humboldt squid flickering red and white at 550 m, and a red-lipped batfish walking on its fins over the sand.
0.11maps of the sea and the world
  • A map: press O (or “map”) for a cross-section of this ocean from the shore to the Challenger Deep, every place marked. Click a dot to swim there. Its other tab, “the world”, shows where each one really lives on Earth.
  • A new tour, around the world; search finds animals by where they live (try “Japan”); the info panel says where each lives in the wild.
  • A cookiecutter shark in the twilight zone, its belly glowing. At the whale fall, hagfish: disturb them and they flood the water with slime.
  • The Lost City: white limestone towers up to 60 m tall on an undersea mountain in the mid-Atlantic, where life may have begun.
  • A polar bear swims under the Arctic ice, and a banded sea krait hunts over the reef. On the sand, a coconut octopus carries its shell shelter, and an electric ray lies buried: disturb it if you dare.
  • A giant clam on the reef (it closes if you come too close) and a school of bluefin tuna in open water.
0.10nets, rafts and warnings
  • Bubble-net feeding: three humpbacks spiral up blowing a curtain of bubbles round a herring school, then lunge up through the middle together (about every 75 seconds; listen for the feeding call).
  • A basking shark cruises near the surface, mouth agape. Keys: N switches day and night, X disturbs what you are locked on to.
  • A peacock flounder lies on the reef sand, both eyes on one side.
  • Christmas tree worms on a coral boulder on the reef (swim right up to them), and remoras riding under the whale shark and the manta.
  • By the brine pool: a Venus’ flower basket, a sponge woven of glass, with the pair of shrimp that live their lives inside it.
  • Sea angels, tiny shell-less snails that fly on wing-like flaps, under the Arctic ice.
  • A shipwreck at 30 m, grown over with soft corals and sea fans, a school of glassfish in its shelter.
  • At night only: a swarm of glowing firefly squid rises near the surface (the atlas marks night animals).
0.9dragons and tornadoes
  • A leafy seadragon drifts by the seagrass meadow, and a tornado of chevron barracuda turns over the drop-off past the reef, and a chambered nautilus hangs over the deep reef slope. Under the Arctic ice: belugas (listen for the sea canaries) and a lion’s mane jellyfish, the biggest jelly of all. In the kelp: a pair of sea lions looping on their fore flippers. Off the mangroves: a box jellyfish, with 24 eyes.
  • Adélie penguins fly through the Antarctic krill swarm, which parts round them.
  • A raft of sargassum floats at the surface, a sargassum fish hidden in it: look up from below.
  • A leatherback turtle, the biggest and deepest-diving turtle, cruises off the shelf.
  • At the vents: scaly-foot snails, the only animals known to armour themselves with iron.
  • A pyrosome in the twilight zone: touch it and a wave of light runs along the colony.
  • A cleaning station on the reef: cleaner wrasses working over a giant grouper, in and out of its mouth. On the sand, a blue-ringed octopus: disturb it to see its warning.
  • On the reef sand: a watchman goby standing guard over the burrow it shares with a pistol shrimp.
  • The pufferfish now swims slim, and swells into a spiny ball if you disturb it or swim too close; the octopus hides in the reef's colours and, disturbed, flushes red and squirts ink.
  • In the twilight zone: the cock-eyed squid, one eye twice the size of the other, and the red helmet jellyfish (disturb it).
  • Today's discovery picks something you have not seen yet; share links keep the time of day; the atlas marks night-only animals.
0.8sand, sharks and depths
  • On the reef: blacktip reef sharks and a school of bluestripe snappers; on the sand nearby, garden eels (swim too close and they duck into their burrows) and a half-buried stingray.
  • A sixgill shark circles the whale fall; the sea pigs now walk; the turtle comes up to breathe.
  • How deep is deep? Click "depth" over the ladder for famous depths on one scale.
  • Ride along with Nautile and press C for its cockpit, [ ] to send it to its next stop.
  • Search finds words in the facts too (try "glow", "teeth", "oldest"); the atlas shows each animal's length.
0.7the shallows, and night
  • On the reef: a cuttlefish, a peacock mantis shrimp, nudibranchs and a puffed-up pufferfish. In the kelp: a sea otter and a garibaldi. In open water: a pod of orcas.
  • A rebuilt dumbo octopus; the moon jelly's four gonads now show through its bell.
  • Above the water at night: stars, the moon, and its glade on the sea.
  • Sound follows what is near: a sperm whale clicking (a buzz when it charges), humpbacks singing, a blue whale calling.
  • In the sunlit shallows a soft fill light brings back the reds and yellows close up.
  • A night dive (tours): coral spawning, flashlight fish that only come out after dark, the risen lanternfish.
  • Every couple of minutes the humpback breaches, bursting clear of the sea.
0.6a living ocean
  • Schools part round hunters and divers. Watch sailfish and dolphins slash through the sardine bait ball.
  • The hunt: a sperm whale patrols near the giant squid and charges; the squid dodges, jetting away.
  • Living lights light their surroundings: the anglerfish's lure, the loosejaw's red searchlight, Nautile's floodlights.
  • Disturb them: Atolla's spinning alarm, the vampire squid's inside-out cloak, a glowing sea cucumber. Click open water and the plankton flash.
  • Watch the night migration (in time of day): lanternfish rise 400 m at dusk.
  • From a whale to a microbe: a new tour that zooms through size, every animal at true scale.
  • Hidden worlds: a seamount coral garden, a brine pool on the seafloor, Arctic sea ice with narwhals, mangroves, seagrass and a dugong.
